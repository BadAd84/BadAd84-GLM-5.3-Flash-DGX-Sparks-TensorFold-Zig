## Defaults that changed under us

Nothing we set changed, but the M binary behaves differently on boot and on the wire with our exact launch line:

1. **Every GLM kernel is a dependent launch, not just the matmuls** (7948cc42d): decode launch order and per-kernel latency differ from P with `TF_GLM_PDL` unset in both. The prompt path keeps its chain plain for a prompt chunk (`757989ebe`: `pdl = ks.pdl and rows < wide_rows`).
2. **EXL3 decode runs on the 16-pair item plan** (4e6cdeed9): the P decode path (compact route rows plus the group scan) is gone for our checkpoint, no switch.
3. **GLM's sampler takes the shared rule** (`cuda.sample.Rule`, 3eeffaff9) instead of its hand-built copy; a host test compares the two over a 4,500-setting grid and finds them byte-equal, so replies should not move, but the code path changed.
4. **Lane prices and draft depth follow new rules** (b9833ab74 and the shared/lone price series): the same replies with different round counts at 1 and 4 streams.
5. **Load-time cost rose**: the expert-form race now sweeps both forms five times at ten widths before timing, "about 5.4 s" at TP=4 (fba8432ae), and it still runs with DFlash2 even though its table is then dropped. The startup line changes from `glm: expert form (timed)` to one that names forms per width.
6. **A stale window-cost record is no longer reused across `TF_GLM_KDA_PAD`, `TF_ROCE`, `TF_GLM_PDL` or `TF_GLM_DSEL` changes** (6f68a979a), so our `KDA_PAD=1` and `ROCE_MULTI=1` boots re-time on first boot instead of reusing a table measured under other values.
7. **GLM's prompt store now counts spare pinned room and reusable storage differently on keeps** (8b08765c8), which changes which warm states exist, not what they hold.
8. **Validation tightened on names we already set**: `TENSORFOLD_MEMORY_RESERVE_GIB` (cuda row, 8ddcb75b0), `TF_GLM_INDEX_CUDA`, `TF_GLM_KV_HOST_GIB`, `TF_GLM_RANK`, `TF_GLM_WORLD` (glm rows, 63996caef, c480f048a). A malformed value is now a startup refusal by name rather than a silent default.
9. **The version is 1.3.0**, and `alias_retire = "1.4.0"` is the first release that will refuse `TF_GLM_RANK`/`TF_GLM_WORLD`; `retired(name, version)` is implemented and tested (`environment_test.zig`), with no production caller yet at M.
10. **`--kv-cache fp8` is documented as lossy** (recipe diff): "FP8 is lossy, so its replies differ from the BF16 cache's, and a mode that changes tokens is not qualified", with a diagnostic receipt: teacher-forced over 360,418 positions, mean NLL within 0.06%, best token the same at 95.5% of positions, KL over the top 20 of 0.016, cache 6,960 bytes a rank against 13,056 (-47%), cold prompts 3 to 5% slower.

## Note on the ranking basis

The `applies: yes` rows are ordered by what I expect them to move, and every size below is the commit's own words, quoted, not a measurement of our stack:

- **The PDL rows** (`TF_GLM_PDL` and the qmmf decode linears) come first because they are the only quantified decode claims in the range: "2-3 us idle between each of its ~1,320 kernels" a step (7948cc42d) and "the PoC measured 1.3% more decode speed" for the linears (85048e5e4). At 1,320 launches a step the idle alone is 2.6 to 4.0 ms, the largest single lever here for a decode round. The prompt-chunk KDA row sits beside them because it bounds the same change on the prompt path.
- **The EXL3 item-plan row** next: "~325 blocks do work instead of ~55k" at 64 rows (4e6cdeed9) is a large change with no tok/s number, so the closest measured size remains our own EXL3_ITEMS cells on v1.2.9, +3.8% at one stream and +5.3% at eight (KNOBS).
- **The pricing rows** (window-cost key, draft depth, shared plain rounds, lone copy/head prices) change round counts and draft choices rather than per-kernel time; our own cells put the draft rules in the 1 to 3% band (KNOBS, `DRAFT_NOISE` cells).
- **The cache rows** (keep admission, `TF_CACHE_EVICT`) carry the only cache numbers, "28-34% fewer prompt rows rebuilt" at 16 GiB (6eeb7c3aa), which matters on the long-context agent shape and only when the cache is under pressure.
- **The transport and prompt rows** (NCCL library, subnet routing, `prefill_many` addressing) are startup and prompt-pass effects with no numbers in the commits.
- **Every later row** is a switch, a registry row, a validation change or a diagnostic with no measured speed at stake; the `no` rows are recorded so that they are known to have been read and rejected, not ranked.
