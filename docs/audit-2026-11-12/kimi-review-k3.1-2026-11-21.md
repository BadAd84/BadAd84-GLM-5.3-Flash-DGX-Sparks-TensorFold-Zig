13. **`COPY_MISS_MAX=7`** - -2.6% TTFT. Dead; copy-agree alone is the only small positive arm.
14. **`TF_GLM_OVN_MULTI_FILL`** - Probe did not contain the several-cold-prompts case and later cells were neutral. **Open only if the real workload batches cold prompts:** test that shape or drop it. Current evidence does not justify enabling it.
15. **CUDA graphs / wide graphs / multi-window** - Dead for launch-gap removal: 99.7% GPU busy leaves nothing to capture. Do not confuse this with PDL, which overlaps dependent work and has a separate 3.34% receipt.
16. **Copy drafts on ordinary prose** - Identical acceptance and throughput, so off for that workload; 0034's NO_COPY addresses code-like matches instead.
17. **Keeping the DFlash2 ring** - Negative expected prize; stale taps, not missing ring state, caused the issue. Dead.
18. **Drafter-ring persistence / minimum resume row** - Same stale-tap root cause, rejected before implementation. Dead.
19. **Register-tiled `LATENT_TILE`** - 1.7x slower than ABSORB_VEC at 94-98 registers and one block/SM. **One explicitly untested variant remains:** G=2 with two blocks/SM. That is an open experiment, not a reason to revive the failed 512-thread form.

## Contradictions and claims to correct

1. **`speed_target.md` is stale as a current verdict.** It reports 39-patch drafts-on values 51.28/59.46/81.42/108.05/134.45 and calls 6/8 streams "at parity" (lines 55-67). Current main is 82.715/85.847/114.272/114.680/125.318, with gaps -0.86/-5.74/-4.75/+2.40/-7.98% against the same target. Keep the production target, replace the Zig table and every inference drawn from it.
2. **The same document says the prompt cache is not part of the gap** (line 94), while queue98 measures 47.40% fewer rebuilt rows and 91.14% lower agent TTFT (s75), and request starts saves 34.51% of a warm turn (s80). The correct statement is that cache reuse was already present at the pin, not that cache policy cannot move real agent turns.
3. **Its drafter conclusion is internally dated and now contradicted by better captures.** The old table has a DFlash2 pass of 26.2 ms against production's 1.1 (lines 127-137), while section 24 has our drafter step at 3.40 ms against 2.3 and section 44 says drafting is level. Do not use the 26.2 ms figure to rank current work.
4. **The "other fourteen are absent" arithmetic and port list are obsolete.** The production-knob table has 19 entries, three marked existing at the pin, so the sentence cannot describe the current set. Several have since been ported or adopted (`DEC_ORDER`, `KEEP_SEQS`, `CHUNK_SOLO`, disk tier, request starts), and upstream #3697 changes KDA padding. Replace the list with the current overlap ledger, not a static absence claim.
5. **Class B of `KNOB-PROVENANCE.md` overstates three situations.** The ten candidates have a 0.86% decode regression, `ABSORB_VEC` has explicit TTFT/decode losses, and `decayed_resume` is NOT ACCEPTED. They are mixed or blocked levers, not "proven faster, not turned on". Move them to a separate "benefit measured, adoption blocked" class.
6. **The adoption record for `DRAFT_NOISE` is incomplete against the repo's own rule.** `prod.env` sets it, while `docs/KNOBS.md` says its 6 and 8 sampled cells are open (lines 30-34). `CHUNK_SOLO` and `HOST_ONCE` likewise lacked direct fresh 6/8 pairs when adopted (s50). A recipe-wide queue91 arm is not a one-knob A/B. Add those cells before calling the five-level proof complete.
7. **Queue99's original reader creates a false 21.91% cold regression.** It averages the `NOTCOLD` 986 ms smoke-warmed row with 4,087 ms to get 2,536.5 ms against 3,991.5 ms (`score99-original-reader.out:1-5`). The amended reader is right to exclude it, but this is a rule changed after the row was inspected: every report must show both, as the current section 78 does, and must not quote the original as evidence.
8. **The queue99 rejection is not a mechanism for decode slowness.** Its three failing cells sit within control spread and each flips sign between pairs, while final cache counters show no different victim choice (s78). The proper statement is "the pre-written 1% rule rejects adoption", not that the policy causes decode loss or that a rerun necessarily settles it.
9. **The `PDL_EARLY` implementation no longer implements its advertised weight-preload variant.** Both `tf_glm_hc_partial8_pdl` and `tf_glm_hc_partial8` call `partial8<false>` (0024:390-402). Section 70 acknowledges the variants were made to enter like plain ones, so this is dead compatibility code, not an optimisation to preserve. Delete the lever rather than citing it as a working early-weight mechanism.
10. **The piece-front mechanism is not settled by the record.** `FRONT_ROWS` was introduced to reduce repeated weight reads after 198/231 extra matmul launches appeared (s59), but joining ranges did not recover time and 2048 regressed (s61). The correct conclusion is that extra launches are not shown to be the warm cost, not that fewer ranges necessarily help.
11. **Some reported model bounds were wrong by roughly 2x and should not be reused.** The 0038 model predicted 6.5% for M against a measured 3.6% cold (s57-58), and 0039 predicted 1-1.5% against a measured 0.86% loss (s62,67). Use measured launch/event cost in subsequent models; do not extrapolate these bounds to 650k.
12. **Restart comparisons are not all like-for-like.** Section 50 itself notes that production's 224.68 s cold reference came from `tier_chain.sh` with load not recorded, while Zig's 291.26 s is a cold reference boot. Use the same-instrument disk-tier table for correctness and restart cost, not the 1.30x speed ratio as though both were one controlled arm.
13. **The 0031 NC1 hot-kernel inference needs profiling, not another receipt citing aggregate tok/s.** `tf_exl3_ovn_nc1` is measured at 75.1% of a four-client verify window, but that workload differs from production's cited `dec4x35k` capture (s54). The percentage identifies our cost centre; it does not prove production uses the same fraction or that an X3 rewrite gains the same amount.
14. **The report should not repeat "no regression at every level" as a blanket statement.** Queue91 itself has greedy code at 8 clients 1.63% slower and marks NOT accepted pending queue95 (s72), request starts has six failures (s80), and `decayed_resume` has three (s78). Every claim must name which arm and which level remains unresolved.

## Bottom line

The remaining speed is primarily prompt-path work and a smaller routed-expert decode kernel problem. The largest immediately supported actions are the re-seat/per-rank KDA decision, prompt-only candidate split, absorb/expand rewrite, rank-0 expert balancing and dense-first-pass attention. The record already contains most of the required evidence; the risky part is promoting mixed results as though they were clean, or quoting the 11 November target as if it described the current stack.
